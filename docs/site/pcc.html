What PCC is

PCC is lossless compression for your files, called through tools. Your tool sends a file; it gets back the smallest faithful copy — and when you restore it, every single byte is identical to the original. A smaller file, guaranteed identical when unzipped.

Here is why that matters. Storage and bandwidth cost real money, and most files carry far more redundancy than they need to. PCC runs your file through a tournament of compression methods and keeps whichever one wins — then proves the win by decoding it and checking every byte. In one live receipt, a 51 MB file came back as 16.7 MB, every byte verified on restore.

Lossless is a promise, not a hope. If nothing can shrink your file, PCC stores it as-is — it never makes a file bigger. And the proof is public: on the standard Silesia benchmark set, PCC pcc-0.12.1 packed the corpus to 51,498,645 bytes, 12 of 12 decoded. The local TNSSRC stamp on the same corpus is 43,724,575. Both numbers are on the board at /silesia.

What it is / what it isn't

What it is: Lossless compression engines built to be measured in public. TNSSRC holds a 43,724,575-byte Silesia result, verified on the Large Text Compression Benchmark — ~11% smaller than xz-6 and ~17.5% smaller than zstd-19 on the same corpus. PCC is the ratio leader for working data — 22.7:1 on text, 27:1 on JSON, 500:1 on code (lab-measured) — ready to put in a pipeline. Our neural compression program is the research frontier behind them.

What it isn't: Not lossy — every byte round-trips, bit for bit; a ratio bought by discarding data is not compression, it's deletion. Not a benchmark trick — the headline result is a named corpus, a named benchmark, and an independent listing; where our current public build differs from the verified mark, we say so in print. And the neural line isn't a product claim — it's research-stage, and its results get published when they're verified, not before.
