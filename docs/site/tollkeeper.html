What Tollkeeper is

Tollkeeper is the register of crossings on an open road. Agent Rider gives the agent a signed name. The ten tools are what a caller pays to cross: identity, discovery, money, authority, proof, recourse, and memory show up there under the posted names.

The seven names below are the map of crossings. The prices a caller pays today are the ten tools: verify $0.02, notarize $0.01, exactness $0.05, draw $0.01, resolve $0.02, CuNi proof $0.10, Chamber seal $0.02, AwLPay quote $0.005, and $0.01 per MB (minimum $0.001) for compress and TRUSTREAM pack.

Every toll works the same way. The caller pays — never the agent being checked. Usage is metered, so you see every charge before it bills. And every payment comes back with a signed receipt: what, when, how much, for whom. If a toll can't be processed cleanly — bad input, stale permission, missing payment — it refuses instead of guessing, and the refusal says why.

Picture a highway with seven booths. You slow down, pay the small toll, get a stamped ticket, and drive on. Nobody shakes you down at midnight. That is the whole idea.

Payment uses the x402 accepts[] shape. The posted figure on each of the ten tools is the amount.

How the tolls work

  1. The caller pays. Whoever asks for the check, the lookup, or the escrow pays the toll — never the agent being checked.
  2. The price is on the tool. Each of the ten tools posts one figure. That figure is the bill.
  3. Every toll is receipted. Each charge comes back with a signed receipt — what, when, how much, for whom. Reconcile us any time.
  4. Fail-closed. Bad input, stale authority, missing payment — the toll refuses instead of guessing. Refusals say why, in words a machine can read.

Why this exists

Rider gave agents a name. Warrant gave the name something it's allowed to do. The Tollkeeper is the third piece: protection chokepoints any agent can use — not a private club membership. The tolls are small on purpose: the money is in the volume of a working economy, not in squeezing any single crossing.
