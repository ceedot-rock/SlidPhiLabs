The method

  1. Exact selection: every candidate encodes for real; the winner is the smallest actual compressed bytes. No estimates, no skipped candidates.
  2. Verify everything: every accepted cell is decoded and SHA-256-compared against the original. Any decode failure invalidates the cell.
  3. Report the misses: losing files, slow encodes, and incomplete cells are reported as-is.

PCC beats the best baseline (xz-6) by 1,178,964 bytes (1.73%). Genuine win: every mode ran for real, the winner was chosen by actual minimum bytes, decode SHA-256 matches the original.

The honest counterweight: PCC took 3,809 s (63.5 min) to encode vs xz-6's 258 s — a 14.8× slowdown for a 1.73% size win. If you pay per CPU-hour, that trade is not always worth it. Decode is fast (36 s).

Baselines only (PCC never completed) on three more files, all SHA-verified: SmolLM2-bf16 xz-6 190,670,328 (0.7087); whisper-base-fp32 xz-6 131,096,048 (0.4514); gpt2-fp32 xz-6 458,414,836 (0.8364). These are not PCC results.

The lab codecs beat the best standard codec (xz-6) by 7,869,766 bytes (5.57%). Every candidate ran for real on every file; per-file winner = smallest actual bytes. 380 verified encode/decode roundtrips, zero failures.

Per-file winners: Coaster 69, PCCX 0.3.0 6 (text/CSV), PCC classic 1. Where the lab loses (honest): xz-6 still won 18 files outright — mostly small CSVs and notebooks, by small margins. The codecs win the corpus by 5.57%, not every individual file.

The honest counterweight: Coaster took ~2.2 h on this corpus vs ~4 min for xz-6. Decode is fast (all methods < 70 s total).

What this page doesn't claim

  • The Silesia 40 MB target is active R&D — no Silesia figure is published on this page.
  • CuNi, Chamber, Agent-Rider, Warrant, and TRUSTREAM are mechanism claims, not numbers claims — no benchmark cells exist for them in the lab, so they don't appear here.
  • Nothing on this page is a "#1 in the world" claim. Opponents are named, deltas are shown, losses are printed.
