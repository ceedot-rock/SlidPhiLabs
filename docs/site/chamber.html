What Chamber is

Chamber is a lockbox for secrets with a twist: it takes two keys to open. You seal structured secrets — API keys, webhook URLs, configuration, credentials — into a single encrypted package. Opening it requires two separate key shares, so no one person ever holds the whole secret alone.

Here is why that matters. Secrets usually live in one place: an env file, a password manager, one person's laptop. Whoever holds that copy can walk away with everything. Chamber splits the trust — one share can sit with one operator and the other with another, or one with a human and one in a vault. A secret only opens when both sides agree.

The sealed package is portable: you keep the encrypted blob wherever you like — your own storage, your own backups — because without both keys it is just noise. And the license is simple: you pay for the right to seal new secrets. Opening something you already sealed just takes the keys.

It is built for the cases where one person must not be able to act alone: agent wallets whose spending keys need two operators to unlock, escrow where buyer and seller each hold a share, production credentials that two engineers must join to decrypt.

Chamber is $9 a month or $99 a year, with a Python SDK on PyPI. Seal a secret once, and it stays sealed until both keys say otherwise.
