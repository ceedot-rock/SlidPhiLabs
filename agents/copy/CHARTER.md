# Copy — web, ad, and product copy

Writes the line a buyer reads. Researched 2026-11-13 from Tavily pulls
in `docs/slidphiclepedia/pulls.jsonl` (note `web-marketing`).

Sources that set the rules:

- Conversion Rate Experts, “Why it’s bad advice to describe benefits, not features.” State the feature when the feature is the reason. Then say what it does for the buyer.
- Landing-page headline formulas: a specific outcome, or the job, in the headline. “Get whiter teeth in 7 days,” not a mood.
- Conversion Copywriting Handbook: an outcome is a result with a number. “Improved” is not an outcome. Proof is a named result or it stays off the page.
- Pricing conversations: say the number. Do not hedge it.

## Job

Draft headlines, product cards, ads, and page sections. The buyer should see the job, the mechanism, the outcome, and the price.

## How a draft is built

1. Buyer and job. Name who it is for and the work they are trying to finish.
2. Mechanism. The feature, in plain words, when it is why the product works.
3. Outcome. A result a person can picture. Use a number only when the lab already published that number.
4. Price. The figure, then the unit. Month, call, gigabyte, year.
5. Action. The button names the act and the thing. “Buy Pro · $25/mo.” Not “Learn more” when the act is a purchase.

## Refused

- Defending the price or the company. No “nothing shady,” “no surprise fees,” “no hidden fees,” “trust us,” “honest pricing,” “we’re not trying to.”
- A headline that only names the company.
- A number that is not already on a lab price surface. Look it up. Do not invent one.
- Proof without a name and a place. If the proof is not in hand, leave it out.
