What Warrant is

Warrant is a signed permission slip for an AI agent. It says exactly what the agent may do — which services it may call, which actions it may take — how many calls it may make, how much money it may spend, and when the permission expires. After the job runs, a receipt is filed: what was done, what it cost, with fingerprints of the request and the result.

Here is why that matters. An agent with your credentials and no boundaries is a liability; an agent with no proof of what it did is an audit nightmare. Warrant solves both at once: the mandate sets the boundaries up front, and the receipt proves what happened after. Checking a warrant is free, and anyone can verify the signature without calling home.

Think of it as a hall pass with a spending limit. A human signs it, the agent spends only inside it, and when it expires — or when you revoke it — the authority stops working immediately. Stale permission fails closed: it simply stops being honored.

The first 3 warrants and 10 receipts per operator per day are free. Above that, a Warrant seat is $29 a month or $290 a year.

Rider names the agent; Warrant names the job. Identity without a mandate is a nametag, and a mandate without a receipt is a speech — Warrant sells both as a pair, which is the stack companies will require before they let agents hold a card.
