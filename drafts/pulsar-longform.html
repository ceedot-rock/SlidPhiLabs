What pulsar is

pulsar is the lab's compression ideas in a form you can hold: a free, open-source compressor you download and run on your own machine. No account, no hosted service, no meter — just a binary that makes files smaller and proves it by restoring every byte.

Here is why it exists. The lab's flagship compressor, PCC, runs as a hosted service. pulsar is the hands-on demo of the same thinking — a working example that shows the principles in action on your own files. If you want to see what the lab means by lossless compression before trusting a hosted service with your bytes, start here.

It is genuinely good at the job. On the public Silesia benchmark — twelve files, about 212 MB in — pulsar packed them to 55.7 MB with every file restored exactly. For comparison, gzip at maximum managed 67.6 MB and bzip2 54.5 MB on the same files. The numbers are published and the method is open.

It is free under GPL-3.0 — Linux binary and full source on GitHub, currently at version 2.5.0. If you want to ship it inside a closed commercial product, a $390-a-year exception covers one product.

Think of it as the demo disc: the real show is PCC, the hosted door — but pulsar lets you verify the ideas yourself, on your machine, for free.
